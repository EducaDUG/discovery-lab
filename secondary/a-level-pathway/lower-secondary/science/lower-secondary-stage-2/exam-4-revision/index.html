    Acids and Bases

    What acids and bases are, and what happens when they meet.

    Acids

    Substances with a pH less than 7. Examples: vinegar, lemon juice and hydrochloric acid.

    Bases and alkalis

    Substances with a pH greater than 7. A base that dissolves in water is an alkali. Examples: toothpaste, baking soda and sodium hydroxide.

    Match the key words

    Acid, base or neutral?

    Strong or weak?

    Fact Antacid tablets for heartburn contain bases that neutralise extra stomach acid: neutralisation happening inside your body!

    Neutralisation

    Word equation

    Acid + base → salt + water

    Acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide (that is why it fizzes). Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen.

    Electric Circuits

    A complete path for electricity to flow.

    What a circuit needs
    • A power source (cell or battery).
    • Wires to carry the current.
    • Components such as a bulb, buzzer or motor.
    Key ideas
    • Electricity flows from the positive to the negative side of the battery.
    • A broken circuit stops the current.
    • A battery stores chemical energy, changed into electrical energy.
    • Wires are metal because metals are good conductors.

    Series and Parallel Circuits

    One loop or many branches: how the layout changes everything.

    Series
    • One loop: components one after another.
    • Current is the same everywhere.
    • Voltage is shared.
    • If one bulb goes out, all go out.
    Parallel
    • Branches: more than one path.
    • Current is shared across branches.
    • Each branch gets the full voltage.
    • If one bulb goes out, the others stay on.

    Compare the circuits

    FeatureSeries circuitParallel circuit
    Number of loopsOneMore than one
    If one bulb failsAll bulbs go offOthers stay on
    Current flowSame all aroundShared between branches
    Brightness of bulbsDimmer with more bulbsStay bright

    Match the circuit type

    Series or parallel?

    Fact Old Christmas fairy lights were wired in series: if one bulb blew, they all went out. Modern ones are parallel, so the others keep shining.

    Changing the Current

    Voltage, resistance and current, and how they are linked.

    Three quantities
    • Current (amps, A): the flow of electrons.
    • Voltage (volts, V): the push.
    • Resistance (ohms, Ω): slows the current.
    Ohm's law

    V = I × R

    More voltage = more current. More resistance = less current. In series, resistance adds up; in parallel it is shared between the branches.

    Current comparison

    SetupCurrentWhy?
    1 battery, 1 bulbMediumLow voltage, low resistance
    2 batteries, 1 bulbHighMore voltage = more current
    1 battery, 2 bulbs in seriesLowMore resistance = less current
    1 battery, 2 bulbs in parallelMedium in each branchEach branch has the full voltage
