    Force and Mass

    Pushes, pulls, weight and springs.

    What is a force?

    A push or pull on an object, measured in newtons (N) with a newton meter. A force can start or stop movement, change direction or change shape.

    Mass and weight
    • Mass: the amount of matter, in kg. It stays the same anywhere.
    • Weight: the force of gravity on a mass, in N. It changes with gravity.
    • Weight = mass × gravity (on Earth g = 10 N/kg).

    Friction and Sport

    Resisting forces, balanced forces and speed.

    Friction
    • A force that opposes motion between two surfaces.
    • It can slow things down or give grip.
    • Air and water give air resistance and water resistance.
    • Friction produces heat.
    Balanced or unbalanced?
    • Balanced forces: no change in motion.
    • Unbalanced forces: start, stop or change speed or direction.
    • Forward force > friction: speeds up.
    • Friction > forward force: slows down.

    Pressure

    The same force can press hard or gently.

    Pressure

    pressure = force ÷ area

    Unit: pascal (Pa). More force or a smaller area gives more pressure. Less force or a bigger area gives less pressure.

    Worked example

    A person weighs 600 N. Their shoes cover 0.1 m². Pressure = 600 ÷ 0.1 = 6000 Pa.

    Snowshoes spread the weight over a larger area, so the pressure is lower and you do not sink.

    Variation in Animals

    No two individuals are exactly the same.

    Where variation comes from
    • Inherited: passed on in DNA (eye colour, blood group).
    • Environmental: caused by surroundings (scars, accent, tattoos).
    • Both: height and mass.
    Two types
    • Continuous: a range of values (height, mass, foot length).
    • Discontinuous: set categories with nothing in between (blood group, tongue rolling).
    Natural selection

    In a changing environment, individuals with helpful traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, so those traits become more common. If people choose the parents it is artificial selection (selective breeding).

    Adaptations and Environmental Effects

    How living things fit their habitat, and what happens when it changes.

    Environmental change
    • Natural: droughts, floods, volcanoes.
    • Human-made: pollution, deforestation, urban development, farming, climate change.
    • Changes can reduce food and destroy habitats.
    Habitat

    The place where an organism lives and finds food, water and shelter. When a habitat changes, species may adapt, move or die out. Biodiversity is the variety of living things.
