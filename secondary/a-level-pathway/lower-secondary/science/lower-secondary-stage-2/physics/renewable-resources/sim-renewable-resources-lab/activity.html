MAKE THE CONNECTION

Where does the energy come from?

Renewable systems use natural flows or materials that are replenished over time. Each source has a different strength, limitation, and place in a balanced mix.

☀SUN

Solar

Photovoltaic cells convert sunlight into electrical energy. Production changes with daylight and cloud cover.

Radiant energy → electrical energy
↻AIR

Wind

Moving air turns turbine blades, which drive a generator. Wind is variable, so balance matters.

Kinetic energy → electrical energy
≈WATER

Hydropower

Flowing or falling water turns a turbine. It depends on water availability and river design.

Gravitational energy → electrical energy
♨EARTH

Geothermal

Heat from inside Earth can produce steam or provide direct heating where resources exist.

Thermal energy → electrical energy
◈LIVING MATTER

Biomass

Plants, organic waste, or biogas can be used as fuel when sourced and managed sustainably.

Chemical energy → heat → electrical energy

THINK LIKE AN ENERGY DESIGNER

Renewable does not mean impact-free.

Renewable resources are naturally replenished, but the flows are limited by place and time. Solar panels, dams, turbines, geothermal wells, and biomass systems all need materials, land, water, or careful management.

Look for a mix that meets demand while spreading risk. Ask: What changes if the weather changes?
