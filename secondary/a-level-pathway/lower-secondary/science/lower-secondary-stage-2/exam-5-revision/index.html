    Breathing and Respiration

    Two different processes that work together.

    Breathing

    Also called ventilation: moving air in and out of the lungs. It brings in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide. It is a physical process.

    Respiration

    A chemical reaction in cells that releases energy: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy. It happens in the mitochondria.

    Bodies and Bones

    The skeleton: support, protection and movement.

    The skeleton
    • An adult has 206 bones.
    • Support: holds the body upright.
    • Protection: shields organs.
    • Movement: with muscles.
    Connectors
    • Ligaments join bone to bone and keep joints stable.
    • Tendons join muscle to bone.
    • Cartilage cushions joints.
    • Bone marrow makes red blood cells.

    Moving Bones and Medicines

    How muscles move bones, and how common medicines help.

    Muscles pull
    • Bones cannot move alone: muscles pull them.
    • Muscles contract (get shorter).
    • They work in antagonistic pairs: one contracts while the other relaxes.
    • Tendons join muscle to bone.
    Types of muscle
    • Skeletal: attached to bones, for movement.
    • Smooth: in organs such as the stomach.
    • Cardiac: only in the heart; never tires.
    Act it out: robot arm

    Hold out one arm. Slowly bend your elbow and say: “Biceps contracts! Triceps relaxes!” Straighten it and say: “Triceps contracts! Biceps relaxes!” Repeat three times.

    Measuring Movements

    Particles never stop moving: Brownian motion and diffusion.

    Brownian motion

    Particles in liquids and gases move randomly because they are hit by other particles. It was first seen in pollen grains in water. A higher temperature means faster movement.

    Diffusion

    The spreading of particles from a high to a low concentration. It happens in liquids and gases, not solids. Examples: perfume in a room, ink in water, oxygen moving into your blood.

    Diffusion or not?

    Match the cause and effect

    Fact You can smell popcorn across a room because gas particles diffuse: fast-moving particles bump into each other and spread out.

    Mark some particles and watch them spread in the Particle Lab. Compare cold with hot.

    Think about it

    Why does perfume smell spread faster in a warm room? Particles have more energy, move faster and spread out more quickly.

    Air and Particles

    What air is made of, and why it pushes on everything.

    Air is a mixture
    • 78% nitrogen
    • 21% oxygen
    • 1% other gases (carbon dioxide, argon...)
    N₂O₂
    Air pressure
    • Air particles move fast in all directions.
    • They have mass and take up space.
    • When they collide with surfaces they make pressure.
    • More particles or faster particles = higher pressure.
