1. What Is Environmental Science?

An interdisciplinary field integrating biology, chemistry, geology, hydrology, atmospheric physics, and social sciences to evaluate natural systems and human interactions.

Key Planetary Metrics
  • • Earth Age: ~4.5 Billion Years (22,000× longer than human history).
  • • Spaceship Earth Analogy: Finite isolated resources requiring stewardship.
  • • Ecological Overshoot: Humanity consumes ~1.75 Earths worth of resources per year.
Water Shortage Paradox: Earth's total water volume (~1.386 billion km³) remains constant. The true crisis is the global scarcity of clean, accessible fresh drinking water.

2. Earth's Interconnected Spheres

Earth operates as a single dynamic super-system. Disruptions in one sphere trigger cascading reactions across all four:

Sphere Composition Key Function
Geosphere Rocks, minerals, soil, tectonic plates Physical structure & mineral cycling
Hydrosphere Oceans (97%), Ice caps, Groundwater Climate regulation & water transport
Atmosphere N₂ (78%), O₂ (21%), Argon, GHGs UV shield & thermal retention
Biosphere All living organisms across biomes Converts solar energy into biomass
Cascade Example: Undersea earthquake (Geosphere) → Tsunami wave surge (Hydrosphere) → Destroyed coastal mangroves (Biosphere) → Reduced coastal carbon sequestration (Atmosphere).

3. Ecosystem Dynamics & Energy Flow

Energy flows unidirectionally through food webs, while nutrients cycle continuously.

Energy at Level N = Energy at Level (N - 1) × 0.10

The 10% Trophic Transfer Rule: ~90% of energy is dissipated as metabolic heat and waste at each step. Only ~10% becomes usable biomass for the next level.

Example Chain: Algae (50,000 kJ) → Tadpoles (5,000 kJ) → Fish (500 kJ) → Heron (50 kJ). Apex predators require vast territorial ranges because energy drops exponentially at higher levels.

4. Ecosystem Services

Free natural benefits valued at over $125 Trillion annually:

  • Provisioning: Direct goods (food, fresh water, timber, medicine).
  • Regulating: Moderation processes (climate regulation, storm surge mitigation).
  • Supporting: Underlying processes (soil formation, photosynthesis, nutrient cycling).
  • Cultural: Non-material values (ecotourism, outdoor recreation, spiritual spaces).

5. Population Ecology & Growth Dynamics

Populations change over time based on birth, death, immigration, and emigration rates:

ΔN = (Births + Immigration) - (Deaths + Emigration)
Exponential Growth (J-Curve) Accelerating growth under unlimited resources (dN/dt = rN). Example: Bacterial doubling.
Logistic Growth (S-Curve) Levels off at Carrying Capacity (K) due to resource limits: dN/dt = rN × [(K - N) / K].

7. Criteria Air Pollutants

WHO reports 99% of global humans breathe air exceeding safety limits:

CO (Carbon Monoxide): Odorless poisonous gas blocking blood O₂ transport.
NOx (Nitrogen Oxides): Forms brown photochemical smog & nitric acid rain.
SO₂ (Sulfur Dioxide): Coal burning pollutant causing sulfuric acid rain.
PM₂.₅ (Particulates): Microscopic soot penetrating deep into lung alveoli.
