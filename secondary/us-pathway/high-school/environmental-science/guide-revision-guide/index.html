Level 1: Foundational Principles (Core Concepts & Systems)

Course introduction, Earth's four spheres, energy flow, ecosystem services, and biomes.

1. What Is Environmental Science?

Environmental Science is an interdisciplinary field integrating biology, chemistry, geology, hydrology, atmospheric physics, and social sciences to understand Earth systems and solve environmental challenges.

Key Planetary Scale Metrics
  • • Earth Age: ~4.5 Billion Years (22,000× longer than human history).
  • • Spaceship Earth Analogy: Finite isolated resources requiring stewardship.
  • • Water Usage: Average individual consumes 80–100 gallons/day.
  • • Ecological Overshoot: If everyone lived like an average American, humanity would require 5 Earths.
Water Shortage Paradox: Earth's total water volume remains constant (~1.386 billion km³). The true crisis is the global scarcity of clean, safe, and accessible fresh drinking water.

2. Earth's Interconnected Spheres

Earth operates as a single dynamic super-system. Disruptions in one sphere trigger cascading reactions across all four.

Sphere Primary Composition Key Function
Geosphere Rocks, minerals, tectonic plates, soil horizons Structural support & mineral cycling
Hydrosphere Oceans (97%), Ice caps/Glaciers, Groundwater Climate regulation & nutrient transport
Atmosphere N₂ (78%), O₂ (21%), Argon (0.9%), GHGs UV radiation shield & thermal retention
Biosphere All living organisms across biomes Converts solar energy to organic biomass
Cascade Flow: Undersea earthquake in Sumatra, Indonesia (Geosphere) → Tsunami wave surge (Hydrosphere) → Devastated coastal mangroves (Biosphere) → Reduced coastal carbon sequestration (Atmosphere).

3. Ecosystem Dynamics & Energy Flow

An ecosystem is an open system where living organisms (biotic) interact with non-living physical factors (abiotic). Energy flows unidirectionally while nutrients cycle continuously.

Energy at Level N = Energy at Level (N - 1) × 0.10

The 10% Trophic Transfer Rule: Approximately 90% of energy is lost as low-grade metabolic heat and unabsorbed waste at each trophic step. Only ~10% is converted into usable biomass.

Boreal Forest Example (Taiga - 17 Million km²): Benthic Algae (50,000 kJ) → Herbivorous Tadpoles (5,000 kJ) → Small Fish (500 kJ) → White-faced Heron (50 kJ). Apex predators require vast territorial ranges because energy drops exponentially at higher levels.

4. Four Categories of Ecosystem Services

Ecosystem services provide free ecological benefits valued at over $125 Trillion annually:

  • Provisioning: Direct raw materials (crops, fresh water, timber, medicinal plants - 50%+ of modern drugs derive from natural sources).
  • Regulating: Process moderation (climate regulation, water filtration, mangrove storm protection reducing wave energy by 66%).
  • Supporting: Fundamental processes (primary production, soil formation, nitrogen/carbon nutrient cycling).
  • Cultural: Non-material values (ecotourism, spiritual sacred groves, Japanese Shinrin-yoku forest bathing).
NYC Catskill Watershed Case: Protecting native forests cost $1.5 Billion versus constructing a $6 Billion synthetic water filtration plant—leveraging natural regulating services.

5. Population Ecology & Growth Dynamics

A population is a group of individuals of the same species in a specific area. Population change is governed by the dynamic equation:

ΔN = (Births + Immigration) - (Deaths + Emigration)
Exponential Growth (J-Curve) Accelerating growth under unlimited resources (dN/dt = rN). Example: Bacterial doubling in ideal lab media.
Logistic Growth (S-Curve) Growth levels off at Carrying Capacity (K) due to environmental resistance: dN/dt = rN × [(K - N) / K].
Density-Dependent: Impact increases with crowding (competition, disease, waste, predation).
Density-Independent: Impact occurs regardless of density (wildfires, floods, Mt. St. Helens eruption).

6. Soil Horizon Stratigraphy

Topsoil formation takes up to 1,000 years per inch:

O Horizon Organic Leaf Litter & Humus
A Horizon Topsoil (High Organic/Nutrients)
B Horizon Subsoil (Clay & Leached Minerals)
C Horizon Weathered Parent Rock Material
R Horizon Unweathered Bedrock Layer
Dust Bowl (1930s): Severe topsoil erosion caused by over-plowing led to modern soil conservation: terracing, cover cropping, and contour plowing.

7. Evolution, Speciation & Invasive Species

Natural selection acts on genetic variation within populations. Geographic or behavioral isolation triggers speciation (e.g., Galapagos finches, Hawaiian honeycreepers).

Invasive Species Criteria:

Must be (1) Non-native, (2) Spreads rapidly, and (3) Causes ecological/economic damage. Examples: Kudzu in USA, Cane Toads in Australia, Zebra Mussels in Great Lakes ($120B/yr US economic cost).

Monoculture Vulnerability: Modern commercial bananas are genetically identical clones, leaving the crop highly vulnerable to Panama disease fungal blights.

8. Conservation Strategies & Priority Sites

In-Situ Protection Preserving natural habitats (National Parks like Yellowstone, Biosphere Reserves). Costa Rica protects 25% of its land.
Ex-Situ Preservation Conserving outside natural habitats (Zoos, Svalbard Global Seed Vault storing >1M seed samples).
Spatial Priority Framework:

• Key Biodiversity Areas (KBAs): Essential sites for global biodiversity persistence.
• Alliance for Zero Extinction (AZE) Sites: KBAs holding the last remaining wild population of an endangered species.

9. Energy Resources & Consumption

Non-renewable resources supply ~80% of global energy. Transitioning to renewables is critical for climate stabilization.

Energy Source Primary Advantage Key Constraint / Trade-off
Coal & Oil High energy density Coal accounts for ~40% of global CO₂ emissions
Nuclear Fission Zero operational GHGs (France 70%) Long-lived radioactive waste & risk of meltdowns
Solar & Wind Abundant, zero emissions Weather intermittency & storage demands
Geothermal Baseload stability (Iceland ~100%) Geographically restricted to tectonic hot spots

10. Atmospheric Criteria Pollutants

The World Health Organization reports 99% of global humans breathe air exceeding safety limits (causing 7M premature deaths annually).

CO (Carbon Monoxide): Odorless poisonous gas from car exhaust; blocks blood O₂ transport.
NOx (Nitrogen Oxides): Forms brown photochemical smog & acid rain (Great Smog of London 1952).
SO₂ (Sulfur Dioxide): From coal burning; causes acid rain (damaged 50% of Germany's Black Forest).
PM₂.₅ (Particulates): Microscopic soot entering lungs/bloodstream (Delhi air quality).

11. Eutrophication & Aquatic Ecotoxicology

Over 8 Million tons of plastic enter oceans yearly (Great Pacific Garbage Patch). Agricultural runoff causes eutrophication:

Eutrophication Sequence:

Excess Nitrogen/Phosphorus Runoff → Massive Algal Bloom → Sunlight Blocked from Benthic Plants → Algae Die & Decompose → Bacteria Consume Dissolved Oxygen → Hypoxic "Dead Zone".

Gulf of Mexico Dead Zone: Agricultural runoff down the Mississippi River creates an annual hypoxic zone reaching ~7,000 square miles.

12. Decoupling: Ozone vs. Global Warming

Feature Stratospheric Ozone Depletion Global Warming / Climate Change
Atmospheric Layer Stratosphere (15–35 km) Troposphere (0–12 km)
Primary Pollutants Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), Halons CO₂, CH₄, N₂O
Radiation Type Ultraviolet (UV-B) Filtering Infrared (IR) Thermal Retention
Global Treaty Montreal Protocol (1987) Paris Agreement (2015)
