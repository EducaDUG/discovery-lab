SECTION 1 • LESSONS 1 & 2

Economic Feasibility, Policy Frameworks & Environmental Impact

Understanding market price thresholds, government subsidies, carbon footprints, and carbon debt.

Core Market & Subsidy Concepts
  • Price Threshold: The absolute maximum price a consumer is willing to pay for an item or energy unit.
  • Variable Costs: Expenses that fluctuate directly when production volume changes (labor, raw materials, overhead).
  • Tax Credit: A direct financial tax reduction granted by governments to offset purchasing costs.
  • Deficiency Payment: A direct government subsidy covering the gap between a guaranteed target price and actual market price.
  • 2019 Subsidy Baseline: The U.S. government spent approximately $7 billion on direct renewable energy subsidies in 2019.
Environmental Impact & Carbon Dynamics
  • Carbon Footprint: Purchasing foreign food increases personal emissions due to food transport fuel combustion.
  • Carbon Debt: The structural environmental deficit created when carbon-absorbing ecosystems (like forests) are destroyed via deforestation.
  • Wind Turbine Impacts: While producing zero operational greenhouse gases, local impacts include noise pollution and visual changes.
  • Subsidy Rationale: Subsidies improve the environment, boost the economy, and increase energy security (they are NOT provided to decrease energy security).

SECTION 3 • LESSON 3

Energy Efficiency & Performance Metrics

Measuring work output versus power input, fuel economy (MPG), and thermal water heating hierarchies.

Exam Target Focus
Core Efficiency Definitions
  • Energy Efficiency: The ratio of useful work obtained from a process divided by total power used.
  • Fuel Efficiency: Miles a vehicle can travel on one gallon of gasoline (MPG).
  • Operating Efficiency: Actual production output divided by expected target output (e.g., 500 projected / 1,000 target = 50%).
  • Renewable Share: Renewable energy accounts for less than one-tenth (~8-9%) of total U.S. energy supply.
Thermal Cooking Hierarchy (Least to Most Efficient)

1. Camp Fire (Least Efficient — 90% heat lost to air)

2. Charcoal Grill

3. Gas Stove

4. Electric Kettle (Most Efficient — >80% direct thermal transfer)

SECTION 4 • LESSON 4

Commercialization Potential & The Valley of Death

Navigating R&D funding gaps, pilot demonstration projects, and enabling technologies.

The Valley of Death

The high-risk multi-year funding gap between initial laboratory research/development and full commercial market deployment where innovations often fail.

Demonstration Projects

Pilot projects built to showcase the real-world application, reliability, and market potential of new energy technologies to investors.

Enabling Technologies

Technologies that integrate smoothly within established electrical systems and infrastructure to enhance performance.

SECTION 5 • LESSONS 5, 6, 7 & 8

Global Policies, Carbon Markets & Grid Evolution

The Three A's, Kyoto Protocol mechanisms, Cap-and-Trade, and U.S. grid reliability (99.9%).

Exam Target Focus
The Three A's of Sustainability

Defined in the UN Millennium Statement for global sustainability:

  • Accessibility (Affordable access for all)
  • Availability (Reliable, continuous energy supply)
  • Acceptability (Public attitudes & environmental approval)

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: Attainability is NOT one of the Three A's!

Kyoto Protocol & Carbon Markets
  • Kyoto Protocol: Legally binding international climate agreement for industrialized nations.
  • Mechanisms: Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), Joint Implementation (JI), and Emissions Trading.
  • CER Credits: Certified extra emission reduction credits are the earned output of CDM projects (not a mechanism itself).
  • Cap-and-Trade: Government caps on allowable emissions with tradable allowances.

U.S. Electrical Grid Reliability (99.9%) & The Global Grid

According to the Department of Energy, the historical reliability rating of the U.S. electrical grid is 99.9%. To integrate intermittent renewables globally, planners propose a global grid—an intercontinental network connecting all continents.

SECTION 6 • LESSONS 9 & 10

Developing Nations, Architecture & Model Projects

Energy poverty, World Bank, Buckminster Fuller's World Game, native-to-place design, and piezoelectric flooring.

FINANCE & POVERTY

Energy Poverty & World Bank

Energy poverty describes individuals without reliable electricity access. The World Bank finances developing nations. Key hurdles include lack of funds, missing tools, and establishing local ownership (insufficient global capacity is NOT a barrier).

VISIONARY DESIGN

World Game & Native to Place

R. Buckminster Fuller's World Game matched global resources to human needs. Native to place architecture utilizes local sunlight and wind currents for natural lighting and cooling.

INNOVATIVE TECH & TRANSIT

Piezoelectric Floors & EmX

Piezoelectric flooring captures kinetic human footstep energy. The Emerald Express (EmX) is a model hybrid public transit BRT system located in Eugene, Oregon.
